SOUTH SAN FRANCISCO, Calif. – November 3, 2026 – Denali Therapeutics Inc. (Nasdaq: DNLI) today reported financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026, and provided business highlights.

“Families and physicians are telling us that AVLAYAH is making a difference for children with Hunter syndrome, and the results this quarter reflect that,” said Ryan Watts, Ph.D., Chief Executive Officer of Denali Therapeutics. “The positive responses to AVLAYAH across patients, physicians and payers, and our team’s strong commercial execution, show what is possible for our TransportVehicle portfolio. We are applying what we have learned from developing and launching AVLAYAH as we work with urgency to bring zafinofusp alfa to families affected by Sanfilippo syndrome type A, who need treatment options designed to address disease in the brain and body. We look forward to sharing the Phase 1/2 data, which will support a BLA submission in 2027 as planned. Next year we also expect data from our TransportVehicle programs in Alzheimer’s disease, FTD-GRN and Pompe disease as we build on this momentum across our pipeline of blood-brain barrier therapeutics.”
Third Quarter 2026 and Recent Program Updates
COMMERCIAL PRODUCT
AVLAYAH for Hunter syndrome (MPS II)

AVLAYAH (tividenofusp alfa-eknm) is the first and only U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved medicine in the emerging class of biotherapeutics that leverage the transferrin receptor to cross the blood-brain barrier. Enabled by Denali's Enzyme TransportVehicle™ (ETV), AVLAYAH is an enzyme replacement therapy designed to systemically deliver iduronate 2-sulfatase (IDS) throughout the body, including the brain. AVLAYAH received accelerated approval for the treatment of neurologic manifestations of Hunter syndrome (mucopolysaccharidosis type II, or MPS II) when initiated in presymptomatic or symptomatic pediatric patients weighing at least 5 kg prior to advanced neurologic impairment.

AVLAYAH's U.S. launch gained significant momentum, driven by strong demand from families across the eligible patient population, physicians committed to treating their patients and rapidly expanding payer coverage. Key launch results include:

•$17.8 million in AVLAYAH net product revenue in the second full quarter of commercial availability.

•Fourth-quarter 2026 net product revenue is expected to be $30.0 million to $32.0 million.

CLINICAL PROGRAMS

Lysosomal Storage Diseases

Zafinofusp alfa (DNL126; ETV:SGSH) for Sanfilippo syndrome type A (MPS IIIA)

Zafinofusp alfa is an investigational ETV-enabled enzyme replacement therapy designed to systemically deliver N-sulfoglucosamine sulfohydrolase (SGSH) throughout the body, including the brain, for the treatment of Sanfilippo syndrome type A. In the third quarter, the Phase 1/2 study completed the prespecified treatment duration for the primary endpoint. The study enrolled a total of 20 participants across the pediatric age range. A Biologics License Application (BLA) submission in 2027 seeking accelerated approval is on track.

Preliminary data from dose-finding cohorts in the ongoing Phase 1/2 study of zafinofusp alfa, presented at WORLDSymposium 2026, showed mean reductions across age groups of 80% and 83% at week 49 in the key disease biomarkers cerebrospinal fluid heparan sulfate (CSF HS) and urine HS, respectively, including normalization of CSF HS in some participants and normalization of liver volume in all assessed participants.

Denali plans to present additional data from the Phase 1/2 study at a medical congress in the first quarter of 2027. Start-up activities are underway for a global Phase 3 confirmatory study.

DNL593 (PTV:PGRN) for FTD-GRN

DNL593 is an investigational Protein TransportVehicle™ (PTV)-enabled protein replacement therapy designed to systemically deliver progranulin (PGRN) across the blood-brain barrier for the treatment of granulin (GRN)-related frontotemporal dementia (FTD-GRN). In August 2026, the FDA granted Orphan Drug Designation to DNL593 for FTD-GRN, underscoring the significant unmet need facing individuals affected by this disease. Enrollment in the Phase 1/2 study of DNL593 is complete with a total of 40 participants with FTD-GRN. Data are expected in the first half of 2027.

DNL952 (ETV:GAA) for Pompe disease

DNL952 is an investigational ETV-enabled enzyme replacement therapy designed to systemically deliver acid alpha-glucosidase (GAA) to muscle tissue and the brain by crossing the blood-brain barrier for the treatment of Pompe disease. The current standard of care is enzyme replacement therapy. Progressive motor weakness, respiratory failure and neurologic symptoms remain unmet needs. The Phase 1 study of DNL952 is enrolling participants with late-onset Pompe disease (LOPD), and initial clinical data are expected in 2027.

Alzheimer's Disease

DNL628 (OTV:MAPT) for Alzheimer's disease

DNL628 is an investigational Oligonucleotide TransportVehicle™ (OTV)-enabled antisense oligonucleotide designed for systemic delivery across the blood-brain barrier to reduce tau by targeting the MAPT gene for the treatment of Alzheimer’s disease. Initial clinical biomarker data from the ongoing Phase 1b study of DNL628 in participants with Alzheimer’s disease are expected in the first half of 2027.

DNL921 (ATV:Abeta) for Alzheimer's disease

DNL921 is an investigational Antibody TransportVehicle™ (ATV)-enabled antibody designed for systemic delivery across the blood-brain barrier to target amyloid plaques in Alzheimer's disease. Safety and clinical proof-of-concept data from the ongoing Phase 1/1b study of DNL921 in healthy volunteers and participants with Alzheimer's disease are expected in 2027.

Parkinson’s Disease

DNL151 (LRRK2 inhibitor) for Parkinson's disease
DNL151 is an investigational small molecule inhibitor of leucine-rich repeat kinase 2 (LRRK2) for the treatment of Parkinson’s disease. Denali is conducting the Phase 2a BEACON study in participants with LRRK2-positive Parkinson’s disease, with data expected in the first half of 2027.

IND-ENABLING STAGE PROGRAMS

Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results

Net product revenue was $17.8 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, reflecting AVLAYAH’s second full quarter of commercial availability in the United States following FDA approval in March 2026.

Cost of goods sold was $0.2 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026. Because manufacturing costs incurred prior to FDA approval of AVLAYAH were expensed as research and development, cost of goods sold during the initial commercialization period reflects a lower average per-unit cost as previously expensed inventory is sold.

Total research and development expenses were $106.1 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $102.0 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2025. The increase of approximately $4.1 million was primarily attributable to higher external research and development expenses for TV programs, partially offset by lower expenses for other research and development programs.

Selling, general and administrative expenses were $37.5 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to $35.5 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2025. The $2.0 million increase primarily reflected higher commercial and medical affairs costs to support the AVLAYAH launch, along with higher site and personnel costs.

Intangible asset amortization was $0.8 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to zero for the quarter ended September 30, 2025, reflecting amortization of the developed technology intangible asset recognized upon FDA approval of AVLAYAH in March 2026.

Net income was $70.1 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, compared to a net loss of $126.9 million for the quarter ended September 30, 2025. Net income for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, included a one-time $194.0 million net gain recognized on the sale of a Priority Review Voucher.

Cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities were approximately $1.0 billion as of September 30, 2026.

About the Denali TransportVehicle™ Platform

The blood-brain barrier (BBB) is essential in maintaining the brain’s microenvironment and protecting it from harmful substances and pathogens circulating in the bloodstream. Historically, the BBB has posed significant challenges to drug development for central nervous system diseases by preventing most drugs from reaching the brain in therapeutically relevant concentrations. Denali’s TransportVehicle™ (TV) platform is a proprietary technology designed to effectively deliver large therapeutic molecules such as antibodies, enzymes and oligonucleotides throughout the whole body, including the brain, by crossing the BBB after intravenous administration. The TV platform is based on engineered Fc domains that bind to specific natural transport receptors, such as transferrin receptor and CD98 heavy chain amino acid transporter, which are expressed at the BBB and deliver the TV and its therapeutic cargo to the brain through receptor-mediated transcytosis. In animal models, antibodies and enzymes engineered with the TV platform demonstrate more than 10- to 30-fold greater brain exposure than similar antibodies and enzymes without this technology. Oligonucleotides engineered with the TV platform demonstrate more than a 1,000-fold greater brain exposure in primates than systemically delivered oligonucleotides without this technology. Improved exposure and broad distribution in the brain may increase therapeutic efficacy by enabling widespread achievement of therapeutically relevant concentrations of product candidates. The TV platform has been clinically validated, with AVLAYAH® (tividenofusp alfa-eknm) as the first FDA-approved medicine leveraging transferrin receptor to cross the BBB.
