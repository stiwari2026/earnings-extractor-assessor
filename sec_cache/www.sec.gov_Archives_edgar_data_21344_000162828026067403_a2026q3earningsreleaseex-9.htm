Coca-Cola Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results and Raises Full Year Guidance

Global Unit Case Volume Grew 5%

Net Revenues Grew 7%;
Organic Revenues (Non-GAAP) Grew 7%

Operating Income Grew 12%;
Comparable Currency Neutral Operating Income (Non-GAAP) Grew 8%

Operating Margin was 33.4% versus 32.0% in the Prior Year;
Comparable Operating Margin (Non-GAAP) was 32.9% versus 31.9% in the Prior Year

EPS Grew 8% to $0.93; Comparable EPS (Non-GAAP) Grew 11% to $0.91

ATLANTA, Oct. 27, 2026 – The Coca-Cola Company today reported third quarter 2026 results. “Our results this quarter demonstrate our system’s sustained momentum, giving us confidence in our updated full year guidance,” said Henrique Braun, CEO of The Coca-Cola Company. “By staying close to consumers and executing with agility across our global system, we are delivering quality growth while continuing to invest in the capabilities, brands and people that will fuel our future.”
Highlights
Quarterly Performance
•Revenues: Net revenues grew 7% to $13.4 billion, and organic revenues (non-GAAP) grew 7%, driven by a 7% increase in concentrate sales and 1% growth in price/mix. Concentrate sales were 2 points ahead of unit case volume due to the timing of concentrate shipments.
•Operating margin: Operating margin was 33.4% versus 32.0% in the prior year, and comparable operating margin (non-GAAP) was 32.9% versus 31.9% in the prior year. Operating margin performance included items impacting comparability, as well as currency tailwinds. Comparable operating margin (non-GAAP) expansion was driven by organic revenue (non-GAAP) growth and currency tailwinds, partially offset by an increase in marketing investments and higher operating expenses.
•Earnings per share: EPS grew 8% to $0.93, and comparable EPS (non-GAAP) grew 11% to $0.91. Both EPS and comparable EPS (non-GAAP) performance included the impact of a 2-point currency tailwind.
•Market share: The company gained value share in total nonalcoholic ready-to-drink (“NARTD”) beverages.
•Cash flow: Year-to-date cash flow from operations and free cash flow (non-GAAP) were $12.5 billion and $11.4 billion, respectively.

Company Updates
•Celebrating local heritage and strengthening communities through its brands: Recently, the company reached milestone anniversaries in three important markets: the United States, Mexico and Nigeria. In the United States, the Coca-Cola system marked 140 years of serving consumers and strengthening communities. According to a recent study from Steward Redqueen, the system today supports nearly 1 million jobs throughout the value chain and contributes approximately $85 billion in annual economic activity in the United States. From Trademark Coca-Cola to fairlife, the company’s brands continue to provide consumers with beverages across many drinking occasions. The system celebrated 100 years of growing together with Mexico through a campaign rooted in consumers’ deep, multigenerational connections with Coca-Cola. The campaign came to life through limited-edition packaging, local cultural icons and integrated experiences, generating strong consumer engagement that contributed to volume growth during the quarter. In Nigeria, a 75-year anniversary celebration of Coca-Cola’s connection with generations of Nigerians brought together national pride, music, authentic connection and self-expression through distinctive packaging, creator content and experiential activations. The campaign reached more than 100 million consumers, contributing to volume growth and value and volume share gains for the quarter. Together, these milestones demonstrate how the company combines global scale with deep local roots to create meaningful consumer experiences and drive growth.
•Globally connecting brands to consumers through locally relevant music experiences: The company continues to connect its brands to passion points, with music serving as a powerful global platform to drive relevance and consumer engagement. As an example, across Europe, Coca-Cola and Sprite were highlighted at 60 festivals in more than 20 markets, reaching over 12 million attendees and over 500 million livestream viewers while building direct consumer connections through first-party data. Highlights include uplifting activations at Tomorrowland in Belgium, Rock in Rio Lisboa in Portugal and Coca-Cola Music Experience in Spain. The company drove strong local momentum in Greece by offering Trademark Coca-Cola products in the newly launched 500ml “Supercan” across nearly 20 live music events. In Great Britain, the company bridged music and soccer by partnering with British rapper Central Cee to create an original song for its English Premier League campaign. Together, these activations strengthened brand relevance and contributed to volume growth for the quarter in Europe.

DEFINITIONS
•“Currency neutral operating results” are determined by dividing or multiplying, as appropriate, our current period actual U.S. dollar operating results by the current period actual exchange rates (that include the impact of current period currency hedging activities) to derive our current period local currency operating results. We then multiply or divide, as appropriate, the derived current period local currency operating results by the foreign currency exchange rates (that also include the impact of the comparable prior period currency hedging activities) used to translate the company’s financial statements in the comparable prior year period to determine what the current period U.S. dollar operating results would have been if the foreign currency exchange rates had not changed from the comparable prior year period.
•“Acquisitions and divestitures” generally refers to acquisitions and divestitures of brands or businesses, some of which the company considers to be structural changes. The impact of acquisitions and divestitures is the difference between the change in the relevant metric and the change in what that metric would have been if we removed the amount associated with an acquisition or a divestiture from either the current year or the prior year, as applicable. We generally refer to acquisitions and divestitures of bottling operations as “structural changes,” which are a component of acquisitions and divestitures and also include the impact of intercompany transactions between our operating segments, as applicable. Typically, structural changes do not impact the company’s unit case volume on a consolidated basis or at the geographic operating segment level. In May 2025, the company refranchised our bottling operations in certain territories in India. The impact of this refranchising has been included as a structural change in our analysis of net revenues on a consolidated basis as well as for the Bottling Investments and Asia Pacific operating segments for the nine months ended October 2, 2026. Additionally, in October 2025, the company sold our finished product operations in Nigeria. The impact of this sale has been included as a divestiture in our analysis of net revenues on a consolidated basis as well as for the EMEA operating segment for the three and nine months ended October 2, 2026.
•“Comparable net revenues” is a non-GAAP financial measure that excludes or has otherwise been adjusted for items impacting comparability (discussed further below). “Comparable currency neutral net revenues” is a non-GAAP financial measure that excludes or has otherwise been adjusted for items impacting comparability (discussed further below) as well as the impact of fluctuations in foreign currency exchange rates. The following information is provided to give qualitative and quantitative information related to items impacting comparability. Items impacting comparability are not defined terms within GAAP. We determine which items to consider as “items impacting comparability” based on how management views our business; makes financial, operating, compensation and planning decisions; and evaluates the company’s ongoing performance. Items such as charges, gains and accounting changes which are viewed by management as impacting only the current period or the comparable period, but not both, or as pertaining to different and unrelated underlying activities or events across comparable periods, are generally considered “items impacting comparability.” Items impacting comparability include, but are not limited to, asset impairments, transaction gains/losses including associated costs, and charges related to restructuring initiatives, in each case when exceeding a U.S. dollar threshold. About The Coca-Cola Company
The Coca-Cola Company (NYSE: KO) is a total beverage company with products sold in more than 200 countries and territories. Our company’s purpose is to refresh the world and make a difference. We sell multiple billion-dollar brands across several beverage categories worldwide. Our portfolio of sparkling soft drink brands includes
Coca-Cola, Sprite and Fanta. Our water, sports, coffee and tea brands include Dasani, smartwater, vitaminwater, Topo Chico, BODYARMOR, Powerade, Costa, Georgia, Fuze Tea, Gold Peak and Ayataka. Our juice, value-added dairy and plant-based beverage brands include Minute Maid, Simply, innocent, Del Valle, fairlife and Santa Clara. We’re constantly transforming our portfolio, from reducing sugar in our drinks to bringing innovative new products to market. We seek to positively impact people’s lives, communities and the planet through water replenishment, packaging recycling, sustainable sourcing practices and carbon emissions reductions across our value chain. Together with our bottling partners, we employ more than 700,000 people, helping bring economic opportunity to local communities worldwide.
