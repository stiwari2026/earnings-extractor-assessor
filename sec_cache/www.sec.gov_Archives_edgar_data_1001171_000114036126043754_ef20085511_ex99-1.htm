Broadway Financial Corporation Reports Third Quarter 2026 Results Highlighted by Improved Earnings, Strong Deposit Growth and Stable Credit Quality

LOS ANGELES, CA – (BUSINESS WIRE) – November 13, 2026 – Broadway Financial Corporation (“Broadway”, “we”, or the “Company”) (NASDAQ: BYFC), parent company of City First Bank, National Association (the “Bank”, and collectively, with the Company, “City First Broadway”), reported net income attributable to common stockholders of $392 thousand during the third quarter of 2026, compared to net income attributable to common stockholders of $218 thousand for the second quarter of 2026, and net loss attributable to common stockholders of $24.6 million for the third quarter of 2025.  Diluted earnings (loss) per common share was $0.04 for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $0.02 for the second quarter of 2026 and ($2.86) for the third quarter of 2025.

The Company reported consolidated net income before preferred dividends1 of $1.1 million, or $0.13 per diluted common share, for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $968 thousand, or $0.11 per diluted common share, for the second quarter of 2026, and consolidated net income before preferred dividends and goodwill impairment1 of $2.0 million, or $0.23 per diluted common share, for the third quarter of 2025.

For the first nine months of 2026, the Company reported consolidated net income of $1.0 million, or $0.11 per diluted common share, compared to consolidated net loss of $28.1 million, or ($3.27) per diluted common share, for the first nine months of 2025. For the first nine months of 2026, the Company reported consolidated net income before preferred dividends of $3.3 million, or $0.37 per diluted common share, compared to consolidated net income before preferred dividends and goodwill impairment1 of $38 thousand, or $0.00 per diluted common share, for the first nine months of 2025. This increase reflects stronger operating performance driven by loan growth, deposit growth, and continued balance sheet expansion.

Net income attributable to common stockholders was $1.0 million during the first nine months of 2026 after deducting preferred dividends of $2.3 million, compared to net loss attributable to common stockholders of $28.1 million for the first nine months of 2025 after deducting goodwill impairment of $25.9 million and preferred dividends of $2.3 million. Diluted earnings per common share was $0.11 for the first nine months of 2026, compared to ($3.27) of diluted loss per common share for the first nine months of 2025. Diluted earnings (loss) per common share for the first nine months of 2026 reflects preferred dividends of $0.25 per diluted common share, compared to ($0.26) of diluted loss per common share for the first nine months of 2025.

Third Quarter Highlights
•
Net income totaled $392 thousand, or $0.04 per diluted common share, and net income before preferred dividends totaled $1.1 million, or $0.13 per diluted common share.
•
Net loans increased $116.2 million, or 11.4%, during the first nine months of 2026.
•
Total deposits increased $192.4 million, or 21.0%, during the first nine months of 2026.
•
Credit quality remained stable with non-accrual loans to gross loans at 1.20% and non-performing assets to total assets at 0.88%.
•
Capital levels remained strong, with a Community Bank Leverage Ratio of 12.83%.

Chief Executive Officer, Brian Argrett, commented, “Our third-quarter results reflect disciplined execution of our strategic priorities, resulting in strong growth in both loans and deposits while maintaining solid capital levels. We continue to operate with a disciplined approach to risk management and underwriting as we navigate an evolving economic environment. Looking ahead, we remain focused on optimizing the balance sheet, enhancing profitability, and delivering long-term value for our stockholders.”

Quarterly Results of Operations

•
Net Interest Income totaled $9.9 million, representing an increase of $406 thousand, or 4.3%, from net interest income of $9.5 million for the second quarter of 2026.  The increase resulted from a $1.0 million increase in interest income, primarily due to a $767 thousand increase in interest income on loans receivable as a result of an increase in the average balance of loans receivable and a $170 thousand increase in interest income on available-for-sale securities due to an increase in the average balance of available-for-sale securities. These increases in net interest income were partially offset by a $589 thousand increase in interest expense due to an increase in interest expense on borrowings, as a result of an increase in the average balance of borrowings.

The net interest margin remained stable at 2.64% for the third quarter of 2026 compared to 2.65% for the second quarter of 2026.

The net interest margin declined slightly to 2.64% for the third quarter of 2026 from 2.72% for the third quarter of 2025. Despite elevated funding costs associated with the higher interest rate environment, net interest margin remained relatively stable during the quarter. Management continues to focus on loan repricing opportunities, optimizing deposit mix, and disciplined balance sheet management to help offset funding cost pressures and support margin stability.

1 “Net income before preferred dividends”, “net income before preferred dividends and goodwill impairment”, “pre-provision net revenue”, “efficiency ratio,” and “tangible book value” are non-GAAP financial measures. A reconciliation of these non-GAAP financial measures and the nearest GAAP measures is provided in the GAAP Reconciliation to Non-GAAP Financial Measures tables that accompany this document.
•
Provision for Credit Losses was $668 thousand for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $1.5 million for the second quarter of 2026.  This decrease was primarily due to partial charge-offs taken in the three months ended September 30, 2026 that were previously reserved, partially offset by a specific reserve on a non-accrual loan. At September 30, 2026, non-performing assets represented 0.88% of total assets and non-accrual loans represented 1.20% of gross loans.

The allowance for credit losses (“ACL”) increased to $10.0 million as of September 30, 2026, compared to $9.4 million as of December 31, 2025.

•
Non-interest Income was $496 thousand for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $950 thousand for the second quarter of 2026, representing a decrease of $454 thousand, or 47.8%. The decrease was due to a $450 thousand fee recognized on a New Markets Tax Credit financing transaction earned in the second quarter of 2026.

•
Non-interest Expense was $8.3 million for the third quarter of 2026, compared to $7.5 million for the second quarter of 2026, representing an increase of $855 thousand, or 11.4%. The increase was primarily due to a $431 thousand increase in compensation and benefits expense, a $247 thousand increase in information services expense and a $113 thousand increase in supervisory costs. The increase in noninterest expense primarily reflects continued investments in personnel, technology, and operational infrastructure to support business growth, enhance client service capabilities, and strengthen the Company’s long-term operating platform.

•
Income Tax Expense was $279 thousand for the third quarter of 2026 compared to $330 thousand for the second quarter of 2026.  The decrease in tax expense reflected a decrease of $90 thousand in pre-tax income between the two periods.  The effective tax rate was 20.03% for the third quarter of 2026, compared to 22.25% for the second quarter of 2026.

Year-to-Date Results of Operations

•
Net Interest Income totaled $28.4 million for the first nine months of 2026, representing an increase of $4.0 million, or 16.5%, from net interest income of $24.4 million for the first nine months of 2025.  The increase resulted from a $7.8 million increase in interest income, primarily due to a $5.1 million increase in interest income on available-for-sale securities, due to increases in the average rate and balance of available-for-sale securities, and a $3.4 million increase in interest income on loans receivable as a result of an increase in the average balance of loans receivable. Further, interest on borrowings decreased $1.7 million due to decreases in the average rate and balance of borrowings. The increases in net interest income were partially offset by a $5.5 million increase in interest expense on deposits due to increases in the average deposit rate and balance.

The net interest margin increased to 2.68% for the first nine months of 2026 from 2.65% for the first nine months of 2025, due to an increase in the average rate earned on interest-earning assets, which increased to 4.97% for the first nine months of 2026 from 4.88% for the first nine months of 2025, and a decrease in the cost of funds, which decreased to 3.00% for the first nine months of 2026 from 3.08% for the first nine months of 2025.

•
Provision for Credit Losses was $2.3 million for the first nine months of 2026, compared to $2.1 million for the first nine months of 2025.

•
Non-interest Income was $2.0 million for the first nine months of 2026, compared to $1.1 million for the first nine months of 2025, representing an increase of $970 thousand, or 91.1%. The increase was primarily due to $697 thousand of additional earnings on bank owned life insurance and a $450 thousand fee recognized on a New Markets Tax Credit financing transaction earned in the first nine months of 2026.
•
Non-interest Expense was $23.8 million for the first nine months of 2026, compared to $49.2 million for the first nine months of 2025, representing a decrease of $25.4 million, or 51.6%. The decrease was primarily due to a $25.9 million goodwill impairment charge recorded in the first nine months of 2025.

•
Income Tax Expense/Benefit was income tax expense of $891 thousand for the first nine months of 2026 compared to income tax benefit of $54 thousand for the first nine months of 2025.  The increase in tax expense reflected an increase of $30.2 million in pre-tax income between the two periods.

Financial Condition Review

•
Total Assets increased by $211.4 million at September 30, 2026, compared to December 31, 2025, reflecting increases in net loans of $116.2 million, securities available-for-sale of $48.6 million and cash and cash equivalents of $38.8 million. The increase in net loans was due to organic loan growth and purchases of government guaranteed loans, and the increase in securities available-for-sale was due to purchases of securities available-for-sale.

•
Loans Held for Investment, Net of the ACL increased by $116.2 million to $1.1 billion at September 30, 2026, compared to $1.0 billion at December 31, 2025.  The increase was due to organic growth and loan purchases.

•
Deposits increased by $192.4 million, or 21.0%, to $1.1 billion at September 30, 2026, compared to $917.6 million at December 31, 2025.  The increase in deposits was attributable to increases of $157.9 million in savings deposits, $33.4 million in certificates of deposit accounts, and $8.5 million in liquid deposits (demand, interest checking, and money market accounts), partially offset by decreases of $4.4 million in Certificate of Deposit Registry Service (“CDARS”) deposits (CDARS deposits are similar to Insured Cash Sweep (“ICS”) deposits, but involve certificates of deposit, instead of money market accounts) and $3.0 million in ICS deposits (ICS deposits are the Bank’s money market deposit accounts in excess of FDIC insured limits whereby the Bank makes reciprocal arrangements for insurance with other banks). As of September 30, 2026, our uninsured deposits represented 34% of our total deposits, consistent with December 31, 2025.  We leverage our long-standing partnership with IntraFi Deposit Solutions to offer deposit insurance for accounts exceeding the FDIC deposit insurance limit of $250,000.

•
Total Borrowings increased $27.0 million to $99.0 million at September 30, 2026, compared to $72.0 million at December 31, 2025, due to additional FHLB advances. The increase in borrowings reflects proactive liquidity management and support for continued loan growth opportunities. The Bank maintains sufficient liquidity and borrowing capacity to support customer needs while preserving financial flexibility.

Asset Quality

•
Allowance for Credit Losses was 0.87% of total loans held for investment at September 30, 2026, compared to 0.92% at December 31, 2025.  Allowance for credit losses was 1.03% of total loans held for investment less purchased loans1 at September 30, 2026, compared to 0.91% at December 31, 2025.

•
Nonperforming Assets increased to $13.8 million at September 30, 2026, compared to $11.2 million at December 31, 2025.

Capital

•
Stockholders’ equity was $255.5 million, or 16.4% of the Company’s total assets, at September 30, 2026, compared to $262.8 million, or 19.6% of the Company’s total assets, at December 31, 2025.

•
Book Value per Share was $11.37 at September 30, 2026, compared to $12.28 at December 31, 2025. The decline in book value per share was primarily attributable to unrealized losses within the securities portfolio resulting from interest rate movements. These adjustments do not affect regulatory capital, liquidity, or the Company’s ability to hold the securities to maturity.  Capital ratios remain strong with a Community Bank Leverage Ratio of 12.83% at September 30, 2026 compared to 14.09% at December 31, 2025.  While capital ratios moderated from prior-year levels, they remain above regulatory well-capitalized thresholds. The decline primarily reflects strong asset growth and deployment of capital into earning assets. Tangible book value per share was $11.24 at September 30, 2026, compared to $12.12 at December 31, 2025.

About Broadway Financial Corporation

Broadway Financial Corporation operates through its wholly-owned banking subsidiary, City First Bank, National Association, which is a leading mission-driven bank that serves low-to-moderate income communities within urban areas in Southern California and the Washington, D.C. market. 

City First Bank offers a variety of commercial loan products, services, and depository accounts that support investments in affordable housing, small businesses, and nonprofit community facilities located within low-to-moderate income neighborhoods.  City First Bank is a Community Development Financial Institution, Minority Depository Institution, Certified B Corp, and a member of the Global Alliance of Banking on Values.  The Bank and the City First network of nonprofits, City First Enterprises, Homes By CFE, and City First Foundation, represent the City First branded family of community development financial institutions, which offer a robust lending and deposit platform.

Net income and diluted earnings per common share before preferred dividends and goodwill impairment represent net income and diluted earnings per share adjusted to exclude the impact of preferred stock dividends and goodwill impairment. Net income and diluted earnings per common share before preferred dividends represent net income and diluted earnings per share adjusted to exclude the impact of preferred stock dividends. Management considers this information useful to investors because it provides additional perspective on the earnings available from the Company’s operating activities and facilitates comparisons across reporting periods.

Pre-provision net revenue is calculated by subtracting noninterest expenses from the sum of net interest income and noninterest income. Management considers this information useful to investors because it provides supplemental information regarding the Company’s ability to generate earnings through its business activities before consideration of the provision for credit losses, which can vary significantly from period to period.

Efficiency ratio represents noninterest expense divided by the sum of net interest income and noninterest income, excluding certain non-core items, as applicable.  Management considers this information useful to investors as it provides supplemental information regarding the relationship between operating expenses and revenue generation.

The Company calculates tangible book value per common share by dividing tangible common equity by common shares outstanding. Tangible common equity is calculated as common stockholders’ equity less goodwill and other intangible assets.

Management believes this measure is useful to investors as tangible equity is a measure that is consistent with the calculation of capital for bank regulatory purposes, which excludes intangible assets from the calculation of risk based ratios, and provides a meaningful assessment of capital adequacy and facilitates comparisons with other financial institutions.
